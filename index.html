Who we are

One community, three addresses

Politics & Philosophy started as a Discord server in March 2019 and has grown into a community of around 1,200 people who argue about ethics, ideology, geopolitics and the future of Europe. Liberals, conservatives, socialists, traditionalists and a healthy number of the undecided share the same channels.

The rule that never bends is good faith. Everything else, including the conclusions, is up for argument. This page is the front door: what we run, what we expect of each other, and where to find us.

The charter

The rules we actually enforce

Every community says it wants civility. What matters is which rule gets enforced when the argument gets uncomfortable. These are ours, in the order we would defend them.

  1. Conduct is moderated. Opinions are not.

    Any position you are willing to defend with reasons is welcome. Moderators referee behaviour and leave the conclusions to you. You can be wrong here, loudly, at length.

  2. Attack the claim, never the person.

    This is the line that gets enforced without exception, because it is the one that decides whether a disagreement produces anything. Swap the argument for the insult and you are done.

  3. The whole spectrum stays in the room.

    A political server that admits one ideology is a group chat with extra steps. Disagreement is the raw material, and we lean European rather than partisan.

  4. Standing comes from turning up.

    Ranks are gated by tenure, not by message count. Counting messages rewards whoever posts most, and in a political space the fastest way to farm replies is to say something inflammatory.

  5. Rooms belong to the people in them.

    Any member can open a voice room and run it: rename it, cap it, lock it, mute, or hand it over. A conversation should never wait on a moderator to be available.

  6. Age is disclosed, so adults can choose.

    Members under eighteen carry a role that identifies them. It does not shut them out of the community. It lets a room host set an adults-only conversation and know that the setting means something.

  7. Some conversations should not persist.

    One channel deletes every message after twelve hours. It is named for anicca, the Buddhist word for impermanence. People think out loud there in a way they will not when the log is forever.

The name

Polisphilo

Polis is the Greek city, the unit of political life that Aristotle thought a person could only become fully human inside. Philo is the loving. Put them together and you get the short form of what the community is: an affection for the business of living together and arguing about how.

The mark is a column split blue and red, the two halves of an argument holding up the same roof. If you need it for a listing or a write-up, the logo files are here and here, and the name is written Politics & Philosophy in full or P&P short.
