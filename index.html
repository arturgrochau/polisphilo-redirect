Who we are

One community, three addresses

Politics & Philosophy began as a Discord server in March 2019 and has grown into a community of around 1,200 people who discuss ethics, ideology, geopolitics and the future of Europe. Liberals, conservatives, socialists, traditionalists and a healthy number of the undecided share the same space.

The one rule that never bends is good faith. Everything else, including the conclusions, is open to argument.

The charter

The rules we actually enforce

Every community says it values civility. What matters is which rule gets enforced when the argument gets heated. These are ours, in the order we would defend them.

  1. Conduct is moderated. Opinions are not.

    Any position you can defend with reasons is welcome. Moderators referee behaviour and leave the conclusions to you. You can be wrong here, loudly and at length.

  2. Attack the claim, never the person.

    This is the line we enforce without exception, because it determines whether a disagreement produces anything. Replace the argument with an insult and you're done.

  3. The whole spectrum belongs in the room.

    Allow only one ideology and you have built a group chat, not a community. We lean European rather than partisan, and we would rather host the argument than settle it.

  4. Standing comes from showing up.

    Ranks weigh how long you have been here alongside what you contribute. A pure message count would reward whoever posts the most, and in a political space the fastest way to farm replies is to say something inflammatory.

  5. Rooms belong to the people in them.

    Any member can open a voice room and run it: rename it, cap it, lock it, mute, or hand it over. A conversation should never wait on a moderator to be available.

  6. Age is disclosed so adults can choose.

    Members under eighteen have a role that identifies them. It does not shut them out of the community. It lets a room host make a conversation adults-only and know that the setting means something.

  7. Some conversations should not persist.

    One channel deletes every message after twelve hours. It is named for anicca, the Buddhist word for impermanence. People think out loud there in ways they wouldn't when the log is permanent.

The name

Polisphilo

Polis is the Greek city, the unit of political life that Aristotle thought a person could only become fully human inside. Philo is the loving. Together they name what the community runs on: a care for the business of living together, and for working out how.

Alfred Adler had a word for that disposition. Gemeinschaftsgefühl, usually translated as social interest, is the concern that reaches past your own case to the people you share a world with, and Adler treated how far it reaches as the measure of a person's health. Politics is where that concern gets argued over instead of simply felt. Philosophy is how you find out whether it survives a reason. We put them in that order on purpose, which is why an argument about this week's election keeps ending up in Aristotle.
